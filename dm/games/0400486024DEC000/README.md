# The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (`0400486024DEC000`)

A Switch 2 game: update v131072, SDK 22.2.8 (`Ounce`). Nintendo EPD's engine (sead, agl, gsys, Havok, CRIWARE; project `Owl`, with Tears of the Kingdom's file layout) on NVN2. Modules `main`, `subsdk0` (NintendoSDK_movie) and `sdk`; one rewriter patch, the frame step ([`patches.py`](patches.py)). Status: plays on the Mac through the title, the opening, a new file's first scene, Link's house and Kokiri Forest, with saves, at about 59 fps with the picture close to console video. Mac only so far.

## What it needs

| Setting | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `mem_reserve 0x320000000`, `mem_alias 0x100000000`, `mem_stack 0x10000000`, `app_pool 0x240000000` | [`game.tsv`](game.tsv) | the game asks `SetHeapSize` for 0x18ce00000; the rest as Donkey Kong Bananza, the Switch 2's 9 GB |
| `mem_map_limit on` | [`game.tsv`](game.tsv) | a map past the application pool fails with ResultLimitReached, as on Horizon. The SDK's allocator keeps freed pages mapped until a map fails, then gives them back ([PR 1444](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/1444) reports a black screen leaving the Deku Tree without it; not reached here) |
| `sys_resource 0x3000000` | `game.tsv` | 48 MB, as the NPDM declares |
| `render_cond on` | `game.tsv` | agl clears a copy of the prepass depth again under a render condition the console skips. Run unconditionally, the opaque pass's `EQUAL` test fails everywhere ([PR 1299](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/1299)'s finding) |
| `gpu_timestamps segment` | `game.tsv` | the game's dynamic resolution decides from GPU timestamps, and with one time per submission it never lowers its resolution ([why](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#dynamic-resolution-that-never-moves-and-draw-passes-that-wait-for-each-other)) |
| `draw_barriers game` | `game.tsv` | about 180 full barriers a frame NVN does not ask for: GPU 25 → 18 ms at full resolution |
| `tex_flush_upload on`, `tex_dirty_upload on` | `game.tsv` | as Tears of the Kingdom: the CPU writes the terrain tiles' height (R32F), blend and hole (RGBA8) maps into textures after creating them. Without the keys those textures stay zero: flat blue holes in the ground around the path with grass floating in them, and grey stone where the console shows dirt |
| `fps_target 60`, `FRAME_STEP` | `game.tsv`, [`patches.py`](patches.py) | the game steps its world by its present interval, which it fixes at 1: below 60 fps it ran in slow motion. The host now decides the interval each frame (60 where frames fit, 30 or 20 where not) and the world follows it ([why](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#agls-interval-controller-a-world-stepped-by-a-fixed-present-interval)) |
| `shader_groups`, `shader_note` | `game.tsv` | shaders grouped as `/Shader/`, `/Effect/`, `/Lib/`, `/UI/` |
| `pad_layout full` | `game.tsv` | a 3D field and both sticks |

## Files

One [NUP container](../../../docs/development.md#the-packing-script-in-detail) of 25.6 GB: `scripts/pack_nup.py` unpacks it to `extract/v131072/`. The RomFS keeps most files as zstd with dictionaries from `Pack/ZsDic.pack.zs`. The movies are AV1.

The build's `Attribute offset 0x5C is not valid` (about 2,300 shaders) is a vertex shader's write to an output the translator drops, probably a shading rate, as `nvnCommandBufferBindShadingRateImage` and its family are no-ops: everything shades at full rate, which is right to look at and costs GPU time. Not verified against the hardware.

## Frame rate

### Game speed and the frame step (current)

The game steps its world one 1/60 s step a frame: agl's interval controller holds the present interval at 1, and the game's frame manager steps the world by 60 / that interval's fps ([how](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#agls-interval-controller-a-world-stepped-by-a-fixed-present-interval)). So below 60 fps the world ran slower: on an Apple M4 MacBook Air (16 GB, fanless) at 27–32 fps outdoors Link ran and jumped at half speed. With the frame step (`fps_target 60`, the stub in `patches.py`) the host sets the interval each frame and the world follows it. ### Frame generation (`DM_FRAMEGEN`, off by default)

- Current (2026-11-07, M4 Air): `DM_FRAMEGEN=blend` or `metalfx` shows a generated picture on the first tick of each frame held 2 vsyncs and the real one on the second ([how](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#frame-generation-between-frames-held-two-vsyncs)): 60 pictures a second on screen at 30 game fps, each real frame 16.7 ms later. In Link's house at a 30 cap, three runs each: off 30.0 / 29.9 / 30.0 fps; `metalfx` 30.0 / 29.6 / 29.8, frame p95 35.7–37.1 ms, 7.9 ms of GPU per generated picture; `blend` 27.3 / 30.0 / 27.4 with frame p95 72–78 ms in two runs (open). Enqueue to display: about 75 ms for the generated picture, 92 for the real one, against 104–108 ms without generation at the 30 Hz link. Not looked at by eye in motion; the screen captures in the work folder (`m-run/screen_n_fg4*`) show no breakage at rest.
- The interpolator gets colour only. What else it could take, from an outdoor frame's pass dump: the scene's depth is there (D32F, 683x384), and agl's depth-linearisation pass (`agl_common.sharcb`, fragment `247836a7c2c52254`, writing an R32F copy) has the camera's near plane, far plane and aspect ratio at the start of its uniform block (0.1, 25000, 1.778): a lead for the camera, in agl's shared library rather than this game's code. Motion vectors: none. The game never calls NVN2's DLSS, and its upscaler to the 2048x1152 picture is `agl_ndsr_vr_light` compute (`a335c1db6270c355`, then `d99fbeeb53d38847` writing the picture), which reads the scene colour and a 683x384 intermediate the earlier `agl_ndsr_vr` passes made, no velocity. The other path is the matrices (macOS 27: worldToView / viewToClip, from which MetalFX derives the camera's motion), from the scene's uniform block. The UI: drawn into the 2048x1152 picture after the upscaler (UI textures in the next passes), which a last pass draws into the 2560x1440 window texture; the host has only the final picture, so a generated frame moves the HUD with what lies under it (MetalFX's `uiTexture` needs the UI alone: the split is the first pass after `d99fbeeb53d38847` writes the picture). Steps 2 and 3 of the plan (depth and camera, the UI apart) are not done.
- Cost: MetalFX's interpolator works at the drawable's size (2560x1440 on this Mac's window): 7.9 ms p50 / 12 ms p95 of present GPU time per generated picture; on a standalone test 3 ms at 683x384, 3–8 ms at 1280x720, 8–9 ms at 1920x1080 (an idle GPU's low clock makes these vary). Interpolating at the game's picture size before the present's scaling would save about half. On a GPU-bound game this time comes out of the frame's budget: outdoors, where 30 does not hold, it would lower the game's frame rate.

### Measured on an M6

Measured on an M6 Mac with 16 GB, headless, on one walk with camera turns (`walk.sh` in the work folder).

- Current: the title and menu, a movie behind them, are a locked 60 fps (p50 16.67 ms, GPU span 15.6 ms).
- Current: walking the village at the game's top internal resolution, 1536x864: a locked 60 fps, mean 16.67 ms over each settled window. Per 2,700 frames: 0-8 frames over 25 ms and 0-2 over 33 ms. Frame p95 17.8-18.9 ms with no window, as the game's own loop moves around its fence wait; in a window p95 17.8 ms, 3 over 25 ms and 5-16 over 18 ms per 600. GPU span p50 16.5 ms, summed GPU 15.8 ms, CPU 6 ms, about 101 passes a frame. The heavy stretch where the village opens (about 1,560 draws) holds 16.65-16.8 ms by lowering its resolution, then returns to full. The main pass used to sit at 763x429 at the same GPU time: [marked shaders tested depth late](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#a-hosts-own-store-in-a-fragment-shader-moves-its-depth-test).
- Before [pool shaders stopped counting as depth readers](../../../docs/reference/rendering.md#pool-shaders-taken-as-depth-readers): passes 118, 104, 219, 224 in a four-frame cycle, frame times 15, 15, 18, 18.5 ms, mean 16.9 ms. Half the frames rendered the reflection probe's faces with one draw a pass.
- The host's GPU figure adds up command buffers, which overlap, so it reads above the real span: 17.5 ms was never a frame's real time. The real span is `@gpu_end_ms − @gpu_beg_ms` in `DM_NVN_TSV`, or the `first start to last end` of `DM_NVN_GPUPROF`.
- The roughly 1 ms the [wind fix](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#dead-code-removal-dropped-a-coordinate-of-a-multi-output-instruction) seemed to add: 0.66 ms of it is the upscaler, 416 → 1076 µs, because its variants then read what they had lost. It is doing its real work.
- Attachment traffic was not the cost it looked: 254 MB load and 277 MB store remain, and the cheapest passes move 20 MB in about 30 µs. The pass count was the cost.
- Resolved: 25-40 ms frames while the GPU sat idle with the next command buffer committed, 51-75 per 2,700 in a bad run: [the GPU idles with the next command buffer already committed](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#the-gpu-idles-with-the-next-command-buffer-already-committed). What holds Metal's submission queue in the kernel is still unknown.
- The title movie's network takes 15.4 ms of compute a frame here, so the title holds 60 fps with almost nothing to spare, and slower GPUs fall below it: [issue 1375](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/1375), 35 ms on an M1 Max.
- In a run that takes shots, each shot costs its frame 60–100 ms, and `DM_NVN_GPUPROF` and pass dumps slow the frames they cover.
- Open: frames of 100–650 ms when an area first loads (640 first uploads, 321 MB, in one frame of 294 ms; another 640 ms of submit with 68 MB).
- `DM_NVN_TBDR=1` was tried and does not help: p95 19 ms on the same walk, against 17.8.
- The first long run on a new chain is slower (p50 60–114 ms), while `MTLCompilerService` compiles pipelines; the next run is not.
- With a window, the first start on a new chain runs the pipeline prebuild first: 33,190 shader pairs, 691 s on this Mac. A run with no window skips it.
- The opening movie runs at the movie's own 24 fps pace during the story section and cannot be skipped: about 8 minutes on a new file.

## Memory

- Current: on the walk, run from an external drive with a 3.5 GB pipeline archive: resident 10.9-11.4 GB, wired about 9.4 GB, on a 16 GB Mac. `vmmap` and `footprint`: 6.4 GB of guest memory and 3.8 GB of Metal textures and targets, 1 GB of IOAccelerator memory. [Reading the archive into memory](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#a-pipeline-archive-on-an-external-drive-is-read-into-memory) used to add 3.5 GB (footprint 15.0, peak 16.7). `mem_map_limit on`: a map past the application pool fails as on Horizon, and the SDK's allocator then gives back the pages it keeps. Other apps' memory adds to the swap: other games running beside it can push the Mac over.
- This game took the Mac down before the fixes (kernel panic in `IOGPUGroupMemory` at 14.7 GB wired): [raw pool table](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#memory-pools-nothing-gave-an-address-to-stay-out-of-the-raw-pool-table), [textures made only when read](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#shaders-report-which-textures-they-read-through-a-pool), [out-of-memory exit](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#repeated-gpu-out-of-memory-failures-end-the-game).
- Why so much: the game's own memory is about the console's 9 GB application pool, and on the Mac its textures also exist as Metal textures, as Metal cannot sample the guest's block-linear storage.
- Open: render targets that move in the game's pool and change size with its dynamic resolution (1580 → 1579 → 1578 …) get a new Metal texture each time, about 2 GB added to the residency set per 300 frames. It costs about 0.5 ms a frame once settled, so recycling them is a smaller win than the walk, but [a report of a GPU driver panic](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/pull/1299) lines up with this churn.

### For an iPhone or iPad build

Not built or run yet; this is what an iOS build would have to cut, from the Mac numbers above. iOS has no swap, and an app gets only part of the device's memory even with the increased-memory-limit entitlement: Mario Kart World's iPhone run had a 6,143 MiB limit on a 12 GB iPhone ([guest block in sparse files](../../../docs/reference/memory.md)). The Mac's 11 GB resident is about 6.4 GB of guest memory, 3.8 GB of Metal textures and targets and 1 GB of IOAccelerator memory, so a 12 GB device needs about half of it gone and an 8 GB one more. On an Apple M4 (16 GB, this chain, seen-shader libraries) `footprint` at the end of a walk in Link's house read 10 GB: 5.7 GB untagged (the guest's memory), 3.4 GB of Metal textures and targets and 0.65 GB of IOAccelerator memory, while the memory line's `GPU allocated` said 16,024 MB; 11 minutes standing there held 9.5–9.7 GB.

Plan (2026-11-07; nothing installed, no iOS shaders built):
- The devices. iPad Pro 11-inch 3rd generation (`iPad13,4`, M1, 8-core GPU, iPadOS 27.0.1): 8 GB, or 16 GB on the 1 TB and 2 TB models; `devicectl` named the model but gave no memory or storage while the iPad was locked, so which one it is was not read. iPhone 17 (`iPhone18,3`, A19, 5-core GPU): 8 GB by Apple's figures. Neither limit was measured: the repo knows 6,143 MiB for an app with the increased-memory-limit entitlement on a 12 GB iPhone and about 3.4 GB without it on an 8 GB one; by the same share an 8 GB device would give about 4 GB with it (an estimate), a 16 GB iPad more than 8 GB.
- The footprint on the Mac, end of each route (M4 Air, `m-run/fpsplit.sh`, nine runs): guest memory (untagged) 5.6–5.7 GB, Metal textures and targets 3.0–4.0 GB (more in the house than outdoors), IOAccelerator 0.83–0.87 GB, malloc 0.5 GB: 10–11 GB. With the guest's memory in files (`DM_GUEST_FILE=1`, what the iOS app turns on by itself when the game's pool is larger than the memory it is given), the game hung before its title at the first residency commit, and with the profile's CPU-write tracking off it loaded but ran at 0.2 fps, paging 35–37 GB from the files in 7 minutes ([open](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#the-guest-in-files-hangs-ocarina-of-times-first-residency-commit-open)); its footprint there, 4.1 GB at the end, is the Metal textures (2.6 GB of a house not fully loaded), IOAccelerator and malloc, and by the split above a fully loaded scene would leave 4.4–5.4 GB, more than an 8 GB device gives an app. Both are the first item for any iOS build: the app turns the files on by itself when the pool is larger than its memory.
- What each cut saves, measured: handheld mode's targets nothing beyond the run-to-run spread (Metal textures and targets 2.9–3.5 GB against 3.0–3.7 GB docked, same routes). The others need code first and were not measured: render targets aliased as in the game's pool (the biggest lead, below), compressed textures kept compressed, the largest mip skipped on small devices, and CPU-unreadable pool pages released.
- The order of work: (0) make the guest in files work for this game (CPU-write tracking without write-protecting file pages; the GPU's pool reads not paging); (1) build the iOS shader set and an IPA, and read the device's `headroom to the system memory limit` in Link's house: on the 16 GB iPad the Mac's 4.4–5.4 GB that is left may fit; (2) render targets aliased in a Metal heap mirroring the game's pool, which also ends the Mac's churn (Memory, Open); (3) block-compressed textures kept compressed and the largest mip of streamed textures skipped, behind a profile or launcher setting for 8 GB devices.
- The GPU: the M4 Air (10-core GPU) does not hold 30 outdoors (34 ms a frame flat out, 30–34 in handheld mode). By core counts and clocks the M1's GPU is about 0.55–0.65 of it and the A19's about half, before the phone's thermal cap halves it again ([A thermally serious iPhone holds 30 fps](../../../docs/reference/frame-rate.md#a-thermally-serious-iphone-holds-30-fps)): outdoors 50–60 ms a frame on the iPad and more on the iPhone, so the frame step's 20 fps (game speed right) is what to expect there, and 30 would need the frame's GPU work about halved: Foliage's mesh draws (a fifth to a quarter of the outdoor frame, a translator change), handheld mode (−15% GPU outdoors), and the game's shading-rate image, which the host ignores. In the house (25–32 ms here) 20–30 fps. Frame generation: `MTLFXFrameInterpolatorDescriptor supportsDevice:` was not checked on either device; on the M4 it costs 3–9 ms of GPU per picture, which a GPU-bound phone does not have.

Candidates, none tried:
- **Handheld mode.** Measured 2026-11-07: no memory saved beyond the spread (see the plan above). The game renders its scene smaller in handheld mode (GPU p50 25 ms against 29 ms docked on an M4 Pro, aCidcOde in [issue 1326](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/1326#issuecomment-6670563063)); its targets are part of the 3.8 GB. The memory it saves is not measured.
- **Render targets aliased as in the game's pool.** The game describes the storage of one GPU-only pool (flags 0x2a1) as many render targets: walking in Link's house, 5,409 new Metal textures (23.7 GB in all) in 3,300 frames, all COMPRESSIBLE 2D targets of 543 descriptions over 819 offsets. On NVN they share the pool's memory; here each description at each offset is a Metal texture of its own. A Metal placement heap that mirrors the pool, with textures at overlapping offsets aliasing, would bound the targets by the pool's size and remove the churn in the Open item above. `image_texture` already does this for one case (resolution families at one offset); the work is mapping NVN offsets to Metal's sizes and alignments. Keeping evicted targets for reuse instead was tried on oot-clean and made the walk slower there: it kept 370 MB more, and both of its runs fell to about 62 ms a frame against 28 ms without it (stan69b/nxnative a3df77e4; measured with the `GPU allocated` figure, which reads high on this game, see the Memory notes above).
- **Guest pages the CPU cannot read.** A CPU_NO_ACCESS pool's textures exist twice, in guest memory and as Metal textures, which costs twice on iOS. Releasing those guest pages after upload saves memory only where no shader reads the range as raw global memory ([textures made only when read](../../../docs/reference/memory.md#shaders-report-which-textures-they-read-through-a-pool) lists why it is not done).
- **Smaller textures on small devices.** Skipping the largest mip level of large streamed textures takes each to about a quarter, at a visible cost; a profile key for small devices, general.
- **Block-compressed textures kept compressed.** To check on the device: a format it cannot sample, decompressed to RGBA8, takes 4 to 8 times the memory.
- **The guest's 6.2 GB heap.** Only the pages it touches count; `mem_map_limit` gives freed ones back. How much of it the game dirties is not measured.

First step for an iOS build: a `vmmap -summary` / `footprint` split of a long Mac run, then the aliasing above, which also helps the Mac.

## Open

- Open: 2 of about 42,850 shaders do not compile (`1f78730053feb036`, `e172bd7ec218ab8e`: `bracket nesting level exceeded maximum of 256`). Neither has shown up in a run so far.
- Open: [the movie network](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/1375) takes 35 ms a frame on an M1 Max, so movies and the title run at about 28 fps there. It fits on this M6, with about 1 ms to spare.
- Current: against a frame of console video of Navi's close-up the picture matches: hair and sign within 1–2 % in red and green. The causes, each general: [float atomics](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#float-atomics-in-storage-memory) (too bright, yellow skin, a pale veil: the exposure read back from buffers that stayed 0), the terrain's CPU-written maps (holes, wrong materials; the profile keys above), [the gather offsets](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#texturegatheroffsets-and-a-loop-over-its-offsets) (the dotted rows in blurred areas, born in the NDSR upscaler, three pictures from one input), and [image atomics on RGBA8](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#image-atomics-on-a-32-bit-colour-texture) (the anti-aliasing's blend weights).
- Current: the grass no longer flickers. Its blades shifted at 10 Hz because the wind's pressure solve read one neighbour at an undefined row ([dead-code removal](../../../docs/reference/shaders.md#dead-code-removal-dropped-a-coordinate-of-a-multi-output-instruction)), so the velocity field (512x512 RGBA16F, a two-buffer ring) held strong stripes up to ±11 with a 6-frame cycle. In the moss on the bridge, the mean difference between adjacent frames fell from 5.97 to 0.70 of 255, and it now grows smoothly with the lag.
- Open, small differences from that same console frame: Navi's core has a harder edge and a narrower glow here (the bloom texture peaks at 0.44; the ball's HDR is about 0.9 with a tiny spot at 45); shadows are less blue, and the far grass about 1.4 times brighter. One frame of compressed video is weak evidence for either. The falling leaves look dark near the camera, not compared.
- A red `texture type mismatch` on `9b310b3349825e9a` (a UI merge shader): a 3D texture left bound in a slot its active branch does not sample. Harmless so far.
- `nn::codec AV1: a packet refused with 12 pictures waiting` (4 lines a run): the decoder's queue is full and the game retries; movies play.
- The `pipeline archive` red line is [issue 342](https://github.com/seedds/nxnative/issues/342), a limit of this system build. `c1_full.tsv is stale` holds on partial libraries, which no full build has stamped.
- The "Navi" name label in the Great Deku Tree scene was missing on an earlier chain and has not been checked since. Not played yet: the Deku Tree dungeon, combat, later areas. Sound opens with no red line; movies and speech were not listened to. Physics issues reported in PR 1299 were not seen on this route (walking, the ladder, the camera).
- No iOS shader build or iPhone run has been done. Its memory use makes an iPhone unlikely as it stands: [what an iOS build would have to cut](#for-an-iphone-or-ipad-build).
